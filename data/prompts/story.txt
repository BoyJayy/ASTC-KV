When Mara returned to the coastal town, the old railway station was still painted blue. She remembered waiting there with her grandfather on winter mornings, watching the signals change beyond the bridge. Most trains now stopped at a newer building outside the centre, but a few local services continued to use the original platform. The station clock had stopped at twenty minutes past seven.

Inside the waiting room she found a notice about a collection of historical photographs. A volunteer named Elias was arranging the pictures on a long wooden table. He explained that several families had donated boxes from their attics. Some photographs had dates written on the back, while others showed people nobody could identify. Mara recognized the fishing boat in one picture because her grandfather had described its unusual red cabin.

They carried the photograph to the harbour office. The harbour master remembered the boat's owner, although he was uncertain about the year. A storm had damaged the northern pier during that winter, and repairs had continued until spring. This detail helped Elias compare the picture with a newspaper clipping. The clipping mentioned a ceremony for the repaired pier and included a list of local workers.

Mara noticed her grandfather's name near the bottom of the list. She had always thought he worked only on the railway. The harbour master explained that many railway employees had helped clear debris after the storm. For several weeks they used an old freight wagon to move stones from a quarry outside town. The same wagon appeared in the background of another photograph at the station.

By late afternoon the three of them had connected several pieces of the story. They still could not identify everyone in the group photograph, and Elias refused to fill the gaps with guesses. Instead, he attached a note describing what they knew and where the evidence came from. The display would invite visitors to contribute additional information.

Before leaving, Mara walked to the bridge. A local train passed slowly beneath her, carrying only a handful of passengers. She decided to return the following month with her family's letters. The town had changed, but its history was becoming more detailed rather than disappearing. At the station, Elias had found a replacement battery for the clock. Its hands were moving again.
