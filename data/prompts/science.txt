Consider an experiment that measures how quickly a metal rod reaches a uniform temperature. One end of the rod is placed against a heated plate, while several sensors record temperatures at known distances from the plate. Before the experiment starts, the rod and the surrounding air are at nearly the same temperature. The sensors do not all respond at once because energy must move through the material.

A mathematical model describes this process using a field that depends on position and time. The model relates the rate of change of temperature to its spatial curvature. Boundary conditions describe the heated end and the exposed surfaces. Material parameters describe the rod's ability to conduct and store heat. These parameters must be measured or obtained from an appropriate reference for the material under study.

The investigator first checks the instruments. A sensor with a constant offset can make a correct physical model appear wrong. A sensor with a delayed response can change the apparent timing of the temperature rise. To separate these effects, the investigator places the sensors together in a controlled bath and records their responses to a slow temperature change. Calibration uncertainty is kept separate from random variation during the rod experiment.

After collecting data, the investigator compares predicted and observed temperatures. A small average error does not guarantee that the model captures the mechanism. Errors may be systematically larger near the heated end or during the first few seconds. Plotting residuals by position and time can reveal this structure. Repeating the experiment with a rod of a different length provides a stronger test than simply fitting the original measurements more closely.

The investigator also checks the numerical method. A coarse spatial grid may smooth a sharp temperature profile, while a large time step may produce unstable or inaccurate results. Convergence tests compare several grid resolutions without changing the physical parameters. If the prediction changes substantially when the grid is refined, the discrepancy cannot yet be attributed to the material model.

Finally, the report distinguishes observations, fitted parameters, and predictions. The original sensor readings remain available alongside the processed data. The report describes excluded measurements and gives a reason for every exclusion. It includes a trial that disagreed with the model because the heated plate lost contact with the rod. That failed trial helped establish which experimental conditions were necessary for a meaningful comparison.
