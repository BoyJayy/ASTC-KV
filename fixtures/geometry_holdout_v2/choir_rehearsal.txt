The community choir met in a school hall whose curtains had recently been replaced. At the first rehearsal in the changed room, the singers thought their familiar piece sounded unusually sharp. The conductor asked them to keep the same positions and repeat a short passage more quietly. Then she moved only the back row forward. These small comparisons helped the group hear the room without immediately changing the way every singer produced a note.

The altos entered after a long pause, following a phrase carried by the tenors. Several people had been counting the silence correctly but listening for the wrong cue. The conductor asked the tenors to sing their final words clearly, then had the altos speak their own opening words in rhythm. Once the exchange was understandable as a sentence, the musical entrance became easier to remember. The notation had not changed; the connection between the two groups had become more apparent.

At the piano, Ellis kept a pencil beside the score. He marked places where the accompaniment could support a difficult entry without covering the singers. During a quiet section, a heavy chord made the melody harder to hear even though every note of the chord was correct. He tried a lighter touch and listened again. The conductor described this as making room for the phrase, a practical instruction that worked better than asking everybody to become louder.

After a short break, the choir worked on the closing page. The last line appeared simple, but its long vowel required a shared decision about when to release the sound. The singers first watched the conductor in silence as she showed the gesture. They then sang the line once while paying attention only to the ending. On the next attempt, they restored the earlier words and the shape of the full phrase. Breaking the problem into two parts gave them a way to identify what had improved.

Nobody expected the evening to produce a polished performance. The group recorded one complete run so that the following rehearsal could begin with something specific to discuss. Before leaving, the conductor wrote three brief reminders on the board: listen for the preceding words, leave space in the accompaniment, and agree on the final release. The singers copied the reminders into their scores, folded the chairs, and carried the piano cover back from the storage room.
